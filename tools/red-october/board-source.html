Is the dip real, and how big?

Fixed cohort of 52 creators trading since before Aug 1 — no roster-composition effects
  1. Real, and far bigger than two days.

    The weekly run rate fell from $74,850 in the week of Sep 14 to $53,320 in the week of Oct 19 — −28.8%, or $21,531 a week, and −21.0% against four weeks earlier. Oct 24–25 is the tail of that slide, not the event. On the correct cohort and after the measured settling lag the two days land at −7.7% and −29.8% against their own weekday baselines.

  2. The 20% figure does not hold, and only one of the two days is unusual.

    Day over day was −20.9% and −26.4%, which is where Ezra's number comes from, but Oct 21–23 ran +13.0%, +19.9%, −3.0%. Against the prior eight same weekdays, Oct 25 is −2.90 standard deviations — a genuine outlier. Oct 24 is −1.06, ordinary Saturday variation. One bad Sunday, not a 20% daily collapse.

  3. It started in mid-September, and the −10% test cannot date it.

    That test fires on 18 of 56 days, 32% of the window — in a declining roster a trailing baseline is breached constantly, so it identifies nothing. The real clock is acquisition: new paid subs peaked at 1,335 in the week of Sep 7 and have fallen every week since — 1,223, 1,115, 1,003, 996, 901, 813. Six consecutive falls, −39%. Revenue peaked one week later and followed. The decline predates the Sept 30 flagging wave.

  4. It is not our marketing, and it is not chatting either.

    Over the five weeks Full Stack fell −27.2% and chat clients, who run their own marketing, fell −30.6% — so it is not our posting. But it is not the chatters either: spend per returning fan is $88.42 against $88.44, exactly flat, average PPV price is +1.9%, and renewals are −2.9%. 88.5% of the decline is simply fewer paying fans — 2,234 down to 1,665. The existing base and the monetisation are intact; acquisition has stopped.

  5. Red October: 33 in the red against a goal of 20.

    Oct 31 projects $271,567 against September's $286,085 (−5.1%); same-window Sep 1–25 against Oct 1–25 is −9.3%. The board's alarm is on — the last three days are 12.7% below the same weekdays a week earlier. Thirteen creators moved tier since Oct 23, eleven of them worse.

Ezra is right, and I had this backwards. I earlier told you the subs were arriving and not buying. Measured against the peak rather than against last week, the opposite is true: new paid subs are −33.5% and the fans who do arrive spend the same as they always did. My −8.1% figure compared two adjacent weeks that were both already depressed — noise on a fallen base.

The decomposition is unambiguous. Of the $21,531 weekly decline, $19,064 (88.5%) is fewer paying fans and $2,466 (11.5%) is lower spend per fan. PPV buying fans −29.0%, PPV sales −31.3%, average PPV price +1.9%, spend per returning fan +0.0%, renewals −2.9%. “Quality subs aren't coming in” should read “subs aren't coming in” — and that is a traffic and funnel problem, which is marketing's, not chatting's.

Link status tracks it, which is the best causal evidence available. Peak week against last week, by Instagram link state: accounts whose link is flagged fell −42.5%, accounts with no link at all fell −25.5%, and accounts with a working unflagged link fell −13.3%. Chat clients, running their own links, fell −30.6%. A broken funnel is not the whole story — the slide begins two weeks before the Sept 30 wave and reaches creators whose links we do not control — but it is the one lever in this list that we own and that moves in the right direction.

No platform change can be evidenced. Two extended searches returned nothing dated Oct 20–26, 2026 for OnlyFans or Instagram. One vendor blog reports updated OnlyFans Terms dated Oct 2026 that lower the payout-hold threshold — effective November, single unverified source, and it cannot explain Oct 24–25. The OnlyFans point stays unconfirmed, and no account whose drop traces to platform gets an action until that changes.
